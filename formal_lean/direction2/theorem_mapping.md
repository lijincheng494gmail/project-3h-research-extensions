# Direction 2 theorem map

The November 19 revised study uses M1/M3 for logistic growth, M0/M4 for weak Allee growth, and M2/M5 for strong Allee growth. Each pair keeps its prey growth law and changes direct predator competition from zero to positive. The matched experiment uses the same `K,m,β,e,d`, units, and competition value across all three pairs. It calibrates one multiplicative prey growth coefficient per law. The large six-model study also contains experiments with different parameter matching.

## How to read the branch hypotheses

`Params.Branch` stores continuous graphs of positive roots on `[0,H]`, differentiability of the growth law at their prey coordinates, and a positive Jacobian determinant on the segment. No branch derivative or between-model ranking is supplied. From the two undifferentiated root equations, `branch_derivatives` obtains both derivatives by taking limits of secants. This also proves that prey density increases with competition. The comparison then uses the intermediate value theorem on the lower prey branch and the two nullclines. The shared starting point fixes which branch is followed even if several positive roots coexist.

The manuscript restricts the stable version to negative trace and positive determinant. Lean proves the comparison over the larger positive-determinant domain. A common positive segment of sinks meets this condition. At `h=0` all three starting coordinates agree. The common segment and the growth gap are essential for the finite claim. The right derivative ordering refers only to `h=0`. At a later point it is the density that is ranked.
